✦ BIG SCREENS, BIGGER CHALLENGES · MSc RESEARCH

Making big screens reachable with one thumb.

Phones keep growing, but the thumb's reach doesn't — leaving a "dead zone" out of reach when you use a phone one-handed. For my Master's at Utrecht University, I ran a study to find which accessibility mode makes one-handed reach the most comfortable.

✦ WHY IT MATTERED

One-handed is how phones are really used.

Most phone use happens on the move, with a single hand and a single thumb. But as screens grow to fit more content, the top and far corners drift outside the thumb's natural arc — a "dead zone" that makes everyday taps awkward or impossible.

Phones ship with accessibility features meant to help, yet it wasn't clear which actually do. This study set out to replace assumptions with evidence — so design choices for reachability can be made on data, not guesswork.

✦ KEY RESEARCH DECISIONS

How I made the study trustworthy.

Decision 01

A gamified 56-square grid

Participants tapped stars across a 56-cell grid using one hand. Turning reach into a game kept people engaged while giving a precise, repeatable measure of where each mode succeeds and fails.

56-square grid prototype
Decision 02

Four modes, head-to-head

Direct Touch, Reachability, Miniaturisation and Track-Pad were all tested under identical conditions — so differences came from the mode, not the setup.

Accessibility mode selection
Decision 03

Numbers and narrative

Quantitative scores told me what happened; grounded-theory coding of participant interviews told me why — pairing statistics with the human story behind them.

Data analysis (Python)
✦ THE JOURNEY

From question to evidence.

01 · Review
A literature review mapped what's known about one-handed use and reachability, and sharpened the two research questions.
02 · Prototype
I built the gamified 56-grid in Figma, with a hand-selection and mode-selection flow so every participant ran the same task.
03 · Experiment
Participants completed the star-tapping game across all four modes, while I captured speed, drop-risk and comfort plus open feedback.
04 · Analyse
Python and statistics surfaced the patterns; grounded-theory coding turned the interviews into the values behind each mode.
✦ WHAT I FOUND

Comfort, not accuracy, decided it.

40%
Miniaturisation won
Rated most comfortable and favoured for one-handed use — ahead of direct touch (33%).
Reachability felt natural
"The most natural and uncomplicated" mode — easy, though it shrinks the usable screen.
Track-pad: accurate, disliked
Safest from drops and most precise, yet judged "slow and unnatural."
Thumb length mattered
Longer-thumbed participants hit targets faster, especially with direct touch and reachability.

The takeaway: the most accurate mode wasn't the one people wanted. For one-handed reach, comfort and a natural feel outweighed raw precision — a reminder that reachability features should be designed around how the hand actually behaves, not just where it can technically tap.
